# Amusement Park Visual Bible

This document is the physical and acting contract for the paired T1 amusement-park dates.

## Shared Place

- The location is a fictional, unbranded Korean urban amusement park.
- It is not a copy of a commercial park, castle, mascot, ride logo, or photo-booth brand.
- The park must feel occupied. Distant anonymous visitors may appear as small silhouettes, but reusable backgrounds never contain a named character.
- The promenade, railings, ride supports, queue paths, and guest-service circulation must remain physically coherent.
- Main story information stays in the upper two-thirds because the VN dialogue panel covers the lower frame.

Runtime backgrounds:

- `amusement_park_parade`: late-afternoon promenade, distant parade and roller coaster.
- `amusement_roller_coaster`: empty front-row point of view climbing a plausible lift hill.
- `amusement_photo_booth`: early-evening two-person four-cut booth beside the park path.

## Calendar Contract

The Namsan and amusement-park milestone outfits are mild-weather clothes. These milestones may fire only in March-May or September-November. If the date-count threshold is crossed outside those months, the milestone waits for the next eligible date instead of showing seasonally false clothing.

Seasonal special dates keep their existing explicit month contracts:

- April: cherry blossoms.
- July-August: sea.
- September-October: fireworks.
- December: first snow.

## Photo Booth Floor Plan

The booth is a rectangular two-adult room:

- The wide bench is fixed to the right interior wall and faces left.
- Camera, preview monitor, and vertical soft lights are fixed to the left interior wall and face right.
- The side curtain is on the near/front wall and is neither behind the bench nor behind the camera.
- The optical axis runs horizontally from the camera to seated faces.
- A camera or monitor above/behind the bench is a hard rejection.
- A single passport-photo stool is a hard rejection.

## Daeun Date: Lost Child

Outfit:

- Daeun: slate-blue cotton chore jacket, ivory knit, dark jeans, charcoal canvas crossbody strap.
- Minjun: worn black crewneck, plain charcoal-black casual jacket, dark trousers.

Acting:

- Daeun looks down at the child, not at the lens.
- The child looks directly up at Daeun and remains visibly tearful/lost.
- Minjun looks at the child and Daeun with a small reassuring smile. His unemployed portrait does not authorize a defeated expression here.
- Daeun's inner right hand holds only the child's left hand.
- Minjun's inner left hand holds only the child's right hand.
- Each adult's outer hand remains in a pocket. Any fused fingers, extra contact, or adult-to-adult handholding is a hard rejection.

The CG appears only after the first paragraph establishes Daeun kneeling to help. If the player returns to rides, the result switches back to the roller-coaster background and portrait instead of leaving the helping CG under contradictory prose.

## Jiyeon Date: Four Cuts

Outfit:

- Jiyeon: wine-red tailored suede cropped jacket, charcoal-black silk mock-neck, fitted black trousers, restrained gold earrings.
- Minjun: the same black casual date outfit used in the Daeun route.

The choice-dependent CG appears only after the player chooses to take the four-cut photos. It must never spoil that result before the choice.

Four-frame acting sequence:

1. Both address the diegetic booth lens; Jiyeon is composed and Minjun has an awkward near-smile.
2. They turn into mutual eye contact.
3. They share a small spontaneous laugh.
4. Jiyeon kisses Minjun's cheek; his surprise is natural, not a distorted scream.

Hands and forearms remain outside every photo crop. Faces, hair, clothes, lighting, seat positions, and apparent age stay identical across all four frames.

## Rejected Results

- Minjun carrying the same hollow expression through kindness, romance, success, and comedy.
- A photo booth whose camera or monitor sits behind the people being photographed.
- Thin outerwear shown in Seoul midwinter or midsummer without an explicit indoor/weather explanation.
- Foreground crowd members with detailed faces that compete with the main cast.
- Daeun with long hair, a ponytail, luxury styling, or teenage proportions.
- Jiyeon with a bob, generic office wear, middle-aged features, or Daeun's soft practical silhouette.
- A family-advertising pose that erases the fact that the child is temporarily lost.
